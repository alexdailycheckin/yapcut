# LinkedIn visuals: the single-image shapes

What ships alongside the words when the post is not a carousel. `linkedin-selector.md`
picks the FEED shape from substance and `linkedin-craft.md` owns the written register.
This file owns the picture.

## The gap this closes

The asset step knew exactly one shape: three or more headed units becomes a document
post, rendered through the kit. Everything else shipped as text, or shipped with a card
invented from scratch that afternoon and never written down.

On 2026-09-07 four single-image cards were built in one day, none of them a carousel,
none of them a template, all four good enough to repeat. The builders lived in a
session scratchpad that garbage collects. That is the failure this file exists to stop:
a shape that works and then has to be re-derived is a shape you own once.

## These are not templates, and the layout is not fixed

The layout comes from what the card has to show, so it should come out different every
time. A quote with the number at the front of the sentence breaks differently from one
with the number at the end. A photograph with a busy top-left has nowhere to put an
eyebrow. A map of six line items is not the map of fourteen.

**What transfers between builds is the reasoning, in four parts:** what the shape is
FOR, what it REQUIRES on the table before it can be built at all, the ONE MOVE that
makes it work, and the way it FAILS. Those are stable. Type sizes are not.

The worked examples record one solution each, with the real hexes and paths, so a card
can be reproduced or read for taste. They are the instance, never the pattern. If a new
card looks like the worked example, that is a coincidence of inputs and not a target.

## Stage 0 of the asset step: which shape

Ask in this order and stop at the first yes. The order is cost, cheapest first, because
the expensive shapes have to earn their day.

1. **Is the post's core a tension your ICP lives with, that a format they already
   recognise carries with no explanation, and is the post's job reach rather than
   authority?** Meme. Cheapest shape in the file by an order of magnitude, which is why
   the second half of that question is load bearing: a meme returns no proof, so a post
   with a measured claim in it should not become one.
2. **Does the post's strongest line belong to someone else, on the record, with a
   number in it?** Borrowed quote.
3. **Is there a named customer, a photograph you took yourself, and two to four
   measured results?** Customer sighting.
4. **Does the post enumerate a whole category, twelve or more named line items?**
   Category map.
5. **Does the post carry three or more headed units of your own argument?** Carousel,
   per the asset step in `linkedin-craft.md`. Unchanged.
6. **None of the above.** Text only. A card that repeats the hook in bigger type adds
   nothing and costs a slot in the reader's attention. Shipping no image is a result,
   not a gap.

**When a post qualifies for two, take the one whose input is already on disk.** A
category map is a day of work. A quote card is twenty minutes. The shape decision is
not a taste call between equals.

## Shape 1: the borrowed quote

**Job: reach.** Someone your ICP already watches said your argument out loud, with a
number, on the record. The authority is theirs, which is exactly why it travels. You
are not the one making the claim, you are the one who noticed it.

**Requires:** a video frame of the speaker mid-sentence, the verbatim line, the
speaker's name and role, and the show. Nothing else, and nothing invented.

**How to think about it:**

- **The frame is evidence, so treat it as evidence.** Whatever proves the quote is real
  stays visible, the show's own branding included. Re-lettering it in your fonts or
  cropping it out turns a receipt into a graphic, and the card then rests on your word.
- **The card carries two voices and they are not equal.** Theirs gets the size, yours
  gets the small type at the edges. Where your mark starts competing with the quote, you
  have turned someone else's authority into your ad, which is the one thing this shape
  was supposed to avoid.
- **Build a reading order where the number arrives alone.** Tiering the type is how the
  2026-09-07 card did it, but the useful part is the goal: the eye lands on the figure,
  then reads outward for the context that makes it mean something. Where the split falls
  depends on where the number sits in the sentence, so it moves on every quote.

**The one move: the big line stays verbatim.** Alex's card reads "around like 35 to 45%
of acquisition", filler included. Tidying that to "35-45%" kills it, because the filler
is the proof that a person said it rather than a marketer wrote it. The line's job is to
sound spoken.

**Failure mode: paraphrase.** If the big line is not a substring of the transcript, the
card is a fabricated quote and does not ship. Same law as the receipts gate, applied to
type.

**Worked example, 2026-09-07.** Matt Swulinski, Head of Growth at Viktor, on 20VC, on
the organic share of acquisition. Alex's personal brand rather than Reach: Bricolage
Grotesque ExtraBold display, Space Mono 700 for eyebrow and footer, ground `#232323`,
accent `#FF5A2A`, ink `#FFFFFB`, footer `ALEXMURESAN.COM`. Frame on top, one accent rule
under it, three type tiers, mono attribution. Built 4:5 at 2000x2500 for the feed, with
1:1 and 16:9 crops kept because the panel was already cut. Only the 4:5 was posted.
Build at `carousels/2026-09-07-organic-mix-quote/`.

## Shape 2: the customer sighting

**Job: proof, and it is the cheapest proof in the file.** You walked past a customer's
product in the world, photographed it, and hung their numbers off it. No studio, no
design assets, no render pipeline. A competitor cannot copy it because the photograph is
yours.

**Requires:** a real photo you took, of a named customer's physical product or premises,
with their branding legible in the frame; and two to four measured results that survive
the receipts law.

**How to think about it:**

- **The photograph is the argument, so nothing you draw may compete with it.** The photo
  decides where type can go, not a grid. Find the quiet regions and put the words there.
  A layout that works on one sighting will fight the next photograph.
- **Name the genre out loud.** The reader has to know they are looking at something you
  found rather than something you made, and one tracked word does that job. Without it
  the card reads as an ad for the customer.
- **The numbers are the second beat.** They arrive after the reader has understood what
  they are looking at, and they want one shared axis because they are a list of
  comparable results. Whether that lands as a band, a column, or type in the picture's
  own dead space is the photograph's call.
- **Do not add the customer's logo.** It is already in the frame. Adding it says you did
  not trust the photograph, which was the only asset the shape had.

**The one move: the photo has to be yours.** A stock camper van is a graphic. A camper
van you walked past is a sighting.

**Failure mode: branding that is not legible.** If the customer's mark cannot be read in
the frame, the picture is a van and the headline has to carry the card alone, which it
cannot. Reshoot or pick another shape.

**Worked example, 2026-09-07.** [redacted customer], three stats (14x revenue from AI search
YoY, 15% to 33% share of AI answers across 77 buyer prompts and 4 engines, +88%
ChatGPT-driven add to cart YoY). Reach brand: mint rule `#2ED3B7`, which is the kit's
own cover edge, over a dark green stat band, Reach wordmark top right, full bleed at
1882x2352, which is the photo's own crop rather than the deck's 2000x2500.
`indie-campers-sighting-FINAL-4x5.png`.

**Open: the display face is unrecorded.** It is a heavy geometric sans and it is not the
deck's Romie. Name it before a second sighting ships, or the second one will not match
the first.

## Shape 3: the category map

**Job: authority, and it is the most expensive shape here.** It maps a whole category as
line items, names the real tools on each, and places you inside one of them. It farms
saves because it is a reference rather than an argument, and it converts because the
reader finds you on the map instead of being told about you.

**Requires:** twelve to sixteen line items, two to four real named tools per item with
favicons, a split that carries an argument, and one figure the map is arguing about.

**How to think about it:**

- **Decide the split before drawing anything.** Core against contested, table stakes
  against differentiator, owned against rented. The two halves ARE the claim and
  everything else is furniture. A single group is a list, and a list does not earn the
  day this shape costs.
- **You are on the map, not above it.** Your block sits inside a group with everybody
  else and is distinguished by weight, because the whole point is that the reader finds
  you in a line item they already budget for. A logo floating over the map is a banner
  ad with a diagram behind it.
- **The figure needs a position that is not a category.** Anywhere it reads as one more
  box, it competes with the thing it is meant to explain. The 2026-09-07 card solved
  that by making it a light source between the groups; the transferable part is that it
  has to sit outside the map's own visual grammar.
- **Density is the point, so legibility is the constraint that shapes the grid.** Many
  small marks in rows is what this shape is, and the alignment decisions follow from
  that, which is where the top-aligned plates ruling below came from.

- **Title it plainly.** Alex's ruling, 2026-09-07, after a hook headline shipped and was
  rejected in his words as "everyone else does something straight". A map is a reference
  and a reference names its subject, so "The organic UA stack" beats any line engineered
  to open a gap. The hook belongs in the post copy, where it can be rewritten after the
  asset is frozen. This scopes the cover rule below rather than contradicting it: a
  carousel cover states the finding because it is page 1 of an argument, a standalone map
  names the category because it is a reference.
- **The figure's source sits next to the figure.** Not in the byline, because the crop
  that travels is the middle of the card. Verify the name before it renders. An
  approximate spelling of a real person is worse than carrying no attribution at all,
  and the person is findable in one search.
- **When the grid leaves a hole, the ask fills it.** Five contested items in two rows of
  three leaves one empty slot, and the ask sitting in that slot reads as part of the map
  instead of a banner bolted to the bottom. Give it the one hue nothing else on the card
  uses. One ask per card, and it has to be the same ask the post makes.

**The one move: the split has to argue.** If the two halves could swap items without
changing the claim, the split is decoration and the card has nothing to say.

**Failure mode: competitor names.** See the rules below.

**Worked example, 2026-09-07.** The organic UA stack map, fourteen line items, built
BESPOKE on the reach-infographic shell rather than as one of its eleven templates,
structure borrowed from the workflows.io "Best GTM APIs" market map. Two dashed
containers, SEO and AEO merged into one Reach block spanning two columns, and the
35-45% figure as a light source between them. Jewel skin: outer `#1B2140`, tile
`#232A4C`, category box `#303864`, sapphire `#6480DC` for core, amethyst `#9E6FE0` for
the Reach block, topaz `#C08A24` for contested, cyan kept on the frame edge, the
headline italic and the figure. Grain off, per Motion below. Plain title, the source of
the figure set small directly under it, and the comment ask filling the empty slot in
the contested group. Reproducible at `~/Desktop/organic-budget-map/` (`build_map.py`
plus `logos/`, with `animate.py` and `gif.py` beside it), verify gate passes.

## Shape 4: the meme

**Job: reach, and nothing else.** A format the feed already knows, carrying a tension
your ICP lives with. It travels because recognition is instant and because agreeing with
it costs the reader nothing. It returns no proof, which is the whole trade: cheapest card
in the file, and it cannot be the asset for a post that has a measured claim to make.

**Requires:** a template your ICP has seen a hundred times, one tension they meet weekly,
and no explanation. If the joke needs a caption to land, the tension is not shared and the
shape is wrong.

**How to think about it:**

- **The joke belongs to the industry, not to you.** The reader laughs because they have
  sat in that meeting. A meme about your own product is an ad wearing a meme's clothes,
  and the feed can smell the difference immediately.
- **Punch up or sideways, never at the buyer.** The 2026-09-07 card puts the LinkedIn
  influencer in the frame and the CFO asking the question, and it lands because the CFO
  is right. Aim the same joke at the CFO and you lose the room you were selling to.
- **Leave the second beat unanswered.** That template repeats the question because nobody
  has an answer, and the silence is the argument. Filling it with your own take converts
  the meme into a caption for your opinion, which is the one thing a meme cannot carry.
- **Match the template's own typography exactly.** A generated template plus your own
  drawn text is two cards in one frame, and the seam is the first thing a reader clocks.

**The one move: pick the tension they cannot say out loud at work.** Everything this
shape does comes from saying the quiet thing inside a format that gives the reader
deniability for agreeing with it.

**Failure mode: mixed type.** memegen auto-sizes every caption to fill its own box, so a
four panel build came back with panel 1 at 61pt against 53pt everywhere else. The fix is
to let the API render only the captions it sizes consistently and draw the rest locally
at matched sizes in the face the template actually uses. Watch the watermark too: memegen
stamps the bottom left, and pasting a patch of the image from just above it costs nothing
against a second generation.

**Worked example, 2026-09-07.** The AI budget card on the Padme template. Panel 1 labels
"YOUR FAVORITE LINKEDIN INFLUENCER 12 MONTHS AGO" over "AI replaces the SaaS stack and
the headcount", the CFO asking "So the budget goes down, right?" twice, unanswered.
1400x1400, Titillium Web Black, 53pt speech and 51pt labels, both matched by hand.
Its builder is a throwaway script in the creator's own workspace, and the one detail
worth carrying over is that it rebuilds from the API every time rather than storing a
base image.

## The few things that are actually rules

Everything above is a way of thinking. These five are not, because they are either
platform facts or calls Alex made out loud.

1. **No competitor names anywhere on a brand-owned card.** Alex's ruling, 2026-09-07,
   set while building the map: AirOps and Omniscient Digital came off it and the content
   line was rebuilt from adjacent tools. A neutral data layer stays the owner's call:
   Ahrefs and Semrush were kept.
2. **Top align logo plates wherever tiles sit in a row**, at a fixed offset from the
   tile top, so plates share one baseline. Centring them drifts with the role line count
   and reads, in Alex's words, "all over the place".
3. **Every card resolves to one number the reader can repeat.** Three equal claims and
   no lead is a card nobody quotes in a comment.
4. **If the card doubles as a document cover, the cover rules still bind.** It states
   the FINDING, not the topic, and its ground matches the deck's ground.
5. **Where a kit has a verify gate, running it is not optional.** A card that overflows
   is caught by the gate or by the reader, and the reader is not a gate.

## Motion

**LinkedIn has no animated image.** Settled 2026-09-07. A GIF uploaded as a feed image
is flattened to a single static frame, and document posts are static. Every moving
graphic in the feed is therefore a VIDEO post sized like an image, autoplaying muted.

- MP4, H.264, `yuv420p`, **limited range**. Capturing JPEG frames tags the file
  `yuvj420p` full range, and LinkedIn's transcode then crushes the shadows on a dark
  card. Capture PNG frames and scale with `out_range=tv`.
- 4:5 at 1200x1500, 30fps, 5 to 8 seconds.
- **The first frame must already be the finished card.** It is the poster frame and it is
  what a viewer with autoplay off sees. Windowing the animation amplitude to zero at
  both ends gets this for free and makes the last frame safe too.

`~/Desktop/organic-budget-map/animate.py` is the reference implementation: one
deterministic `setFrame(t)` drives the whole timeline, so frames are reproducible.

**Texture is a PNG luxury.** The kit paints two raster grain layers over every card and
its catalogue treats both as mandatory. They came off this one at Alex's call: the texture
read as fuzz at full size, and at 256 colours it stopped being texture at all. Measured on
a pure ground strip, noise fell from 7.52 to 2.23 sigma and the flattest patch went to
0.00, which is genuinely flat. Every file got smaller, because flat areas compress and
noise does not.

**A GIF is not a feed asset, and it is still worth building.** It animates in DMs, Slack,
email and on a web page, none of which take an MP4 inline. Four things decide whether it
looks like the card or like a bad screenshot of it.

- **Never downscale.** The first attempt went to 720px wide to hold the file size down and
  destroyed the 16px role labels. Ship it at full 1200x1500 or do not ship it.
- **Never source it from the encoded MP4.** H.264 has already softened the grain and the
  palette pass then quantises that mush. Capture PNG frames once and feed both encoders
  from the same frames.
- **Turn dithering off.** Measured on this card: none at 39.4 dB, bayer at 39.1,
  sierra2_4a at 38.1. Error diffusion reads as speckle on a flat dark ground, and with it
  off the box interiors stop crawling between frames.
- **Confine the motion to one band.** `diff_mode=rectangle` rewrites only the bounding box
  of what changed, so the version where just the sun band moves came out at 2.0MB against
  17MB for the same card with a brightness wave rolling across all fourteen boxes.
  Everything outside the band is written once, which is also why its type stays identical
  to the PNG.

`~/Desktop/organic-budget-map/gif.py` is the reference implementation.

## What is not settled

- **None of the three has a kit template, and that may be correct.** All three are
  bespoke HTML plus a headless render. Templating a shape whose layout is supposed to
  come from its content is how it turns into a skin, so the case for promoting one is
  weaker than it looked: the quote card is the only real candidate, because its input is
  four fields and a cropped panel.
- **The sighting card's display face is unrecorded**, per Shape 2.
- **Nothing here is committed.** This file is untracked in the yapcut clone and the
  plugin cache has no copy, so a session loading outlier-radar from the cache cannot
  see it yet. It ships on a deliberate push plus a cache refresh.
- **Measurement is n=0 on all four.** The claims about what each shape is FOR are
  arguments, not measurements. `log_perf.py` should carry a shape field before any of
  this hardens into a weight.
- **The meme is the one shape where a miss is visible.** A map that does not land gets
  scrolled past. A joke that does not land reads as trying, and it spends more than it
  risked. That asymmetry is an argument for using it on a week with something else
  already working, and it is untested.
